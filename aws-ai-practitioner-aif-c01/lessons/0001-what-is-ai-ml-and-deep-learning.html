What are AI, ML, and Deep Learning?

This certification is a job requirement, so let's go straight to what the exam actually asks: the basic terminology that shows up in almost every other question. If these four terms are solid today, the rest of the course moves much faster.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)
Any system built to make a machine imitate tasks that normally require human reasoning (recognizing an image, translating text, playing chess).
Machine Learning (ML) — a subset of AI
Instead of hand-coding rules, you show the system many examples (data) and it learns the pattern on its own.
Deep Learning (DL) — a subset of ML
ML that uses neural networks with many layers. It's what made large-scale image recognition and natural language possible.
Generative AI (GenAI) — a subset of Deep Learning
Deep learning models (called foundation models) trained on huge amounts of data, able to generate new content: text, images, code. Amazon Bedrock is AWS's service for using them.

It's a Russian nesting doll: each term sits inside the previous one.

AI
ML
Deep Learning
Generative AI

Why this matters for the exam: many Domain 1 questions are variations of "is this AI, ML, DL, or GenAI?". If you know the order of the nesting doll, you can eliminate options even without knowing the technical detail.

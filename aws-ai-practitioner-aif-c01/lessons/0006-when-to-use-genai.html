When to Use (and Not Use) Generative AI

Lesson 5 established that generative AI can confidently produce wrong answers. That's not a reason to avoid it everywhere — it's a reason to be deliberate about where you point it. This lesson gives you the litmus test the exam expects.

The litmus test

Ask two questions about the task: (1) Can a person review the output before it causes harm? and (2) does the task tolerate a "good enough, creative" answer, or does it demand one exact, reproducible answer? Review-tolerant + creative/subjective → good fit. No time to review + needs exactness → poor fit.

Good fits

  • Drafting marketing copy or emails (a human edits before sending)
  • Summarizing long documents for a quick skim
  • Conversational chatbots for general Q&A
  • Generating first-draft code for a developer to review
  • Personalizing product descriptions at scale

Poor fits

  • Calculating exact interest, tax, or payroll amounts
  • Real-time safety shutoffs with no time for review
  • Anything requiring a fully auditable, reproducible decision trail
  • Tasks where a wrong-but-plausible answer causes immediate harm

Notice the pattern: every "good fit" leaves room for a human to catch a mistake, or simply doesn't need one exact answer. Every "poor fit" either removes that safety net (real-time, no review) or demands the one kind of precision GenAI wasn't built to guarantee (exact math, full determinism).

Why this matters for the exam: AIF-C01 loves to describe a business scenario and ask "should this company use generative AI?" — it's testing whether you can spot review-tolerance and precision needs, not whether you've memorized a list.

Practice with exam-style scenarios

1. A marketing team wants to quickly draft dozens of ad-copy variations, with a person reviewing and editing each one before it's published. Which factor makes this a strong use case for generative AI?
A human reviews the draft before it ships, so an occasional wrong or awkward suggestion gets caught rather than causing harm.
2. A bank must calculate the exact interest owed on thousands of loans, with zero tolerance for rounding errors. Which factor makes this a poor fit for generative AI?
Generative AI predicts plausible tokens, not guaranteed-correct arithmetic — exact, zero-tolerance calculations belong to a deterministic system instead.
3. A company is building an automatic emergency shutoff for industrial machinery, triggered instantly by real-time sensor readings. Which factor makes this a poor fit for generative AI?
An instant, safety-critical decision has no human-review safety net to catch a hallucinated or wrong output before harm occurs.
